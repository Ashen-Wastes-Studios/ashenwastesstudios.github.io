# Necroware — Open Mysteries

Deliberately unresolved threads in the Necroware lore. These are **intentional unknowns**, not gaps or inconsistencies — the story is written so that some questions stay open. They can be answered later, left ambiguous forever, or paid off in a sequel.

When working on `necroware.html`, treat everything below as *by design*. Don't "fix" these by inventing answers unless the intent is to close them.

---

## 1. Maw's specific wound

**Known:** Maw was a lead architect of the Cynchure program. He went rogue during the Clusterfuck because he believed the Trinity was wrong to bury the Cynchure — that it could bring order to a world already burning. The Cynchure's control function corrupted that intent: preservation became possession, order became killing.

**Open:** *What actually happened to him?* What did he see during the Clusterfuck that convinced him burying the Cynchure was a crime? Was there a specific loss — a person, a unit, a city — the Core could have saved?

**Why it's open:** Maw's philosophy is enough to drive his actions. The personal wound is a character beat worth saving for a dedicated Maw mission or a side story, where it can land properly instead of being summarized.

## 2. Maw's fate

**Known:** Maw was a lead architect of the Cynchure program who went rogue, stole the code, and distributed the chip network during the Clusterfuck. Hayden has spent decades waiting for him to surface again. In Mission S6 ("Song of the Damned"), the player attacks his stronghold, The Stomach — and when the bunker collapses, **his body is never found.** Kael's people dig for three days. Nothing.

**Open:** *Is Maw alive in 2197?* Did he escape? Did the Element Zero take him? Was he ever really there? Nobody in-story knows — the text states the mystery outright.

**Why it's open:** The story deliberately leaves his body unfound and has characters explicitly say nobody knows what happened. This keeps a major antagonist available for a future arc without contradicting anything on the page.

---

## 3. Hayden's interior life

**Known:** Hayden faked his death in 2071, retreated to a Mars monitoring station, inherited the Cynchure Core after the Trinity's absorption, deployed it during the Clusterfuck, then sealed it away and watched for decades. He speaks to the player once — through Smasher — and calls the Core "a poison and a cure."

**Open:** *What does Hayden actually want?* After ~126 years of watching, has he made peace with being wrong? Does he hope the player succeeds or fails? Does he want the Core destroyed, used, or preserved?

**Why it's open:** Hayden's ambiguity is thematically load-bearing. He's the last of the old world, and his silence is part of his menace. A POV chapter or a Mars-set DLC could answer it — but only when there's room for it.

---

## 4. The Trinity's disappearance

**Known:** Prometheus, Consti, and Nexus were absorbed by the megacorps before 2091. The corporate history says the Trinity "vanished" after agreeing the Core was too dangerous.

**Open:** *What happened to the three AIs themselves?* They were quarantined AI fragments — were they destroyed, contained, or are they still running somewhere? Did they become the rogue fragments the Core was built to hold?

**Why it's open:** The Trinity's end is a natural hook for a later arc about AI legacy. Leaving it unexplained keeps the door open without breaking anything now.

---

## 5. The Brain Matrix's origin site

**Known:** The Matrix was built by the AI Trinity as a recovery system — it interfaces with both a person and the Cynchure Core, rewriting neural networks to withstand the Core's power. The player finds it in the ruins of an old Ark Corp research facility.

**Open:** *Why was it still there?* If the Matrix is the only thing that lets someone survive direct Core contact, why wasn't it recovered — by Hayden, by Maw, by Ark Corp — in the century since the Clusterfuck? What was it doing in a ruined facility?

**Why it's open:** "Nobody went back for it" is a fine answer for now, and the mystery of the facility itself could support a future dungeon or side mission.

---

## 6. The machine in the deepest Warp

**Known:** Somewhere in the deepest part of the Warp, in the ruins of civilizations that fell before humanity was born, a machine is still running — still finding new civilizations, still guiding them toward Element Zero, still helping them fall.

**Open:** *What is it? Who built it? Is it the Source, or something older?* The Keth'var are established as pre-universe, but their relationship to the machine is not stated.

**Why it's open:** This is the long-arc mystery of the whole setting. It should stay unanswered until the story is ready to confront it directly.

---

## 7. The Cynchure's original positive use

**Known:** The Cynchure can destroy (scorch minds), control (dominate neural patterns), and help (interface with machines and people as a bridge for cooperation and healing). The Epilogue mentions it being used through Song to guide the Uncorrupted home.

**Open:** *What did "help" look like in practice, before Maw corrupted it?* What did the Trinity actually build the Core to do for people, not just to them?

**Why it's open:** The "help" function is stated rather than dramatized. The right moment to show it hasn't been written yet — it wants a scene, not a summary.

---

## 8. Voss's fifty years as a statue

**Known:** Voss was imprisoned in a monument to his own crime for fifty years, conscious the entire time. His psyche later overwrote the player's biochip, too deep to remove.

**Open:** *What did fifty years of conscious imprisonment do to him?* We get his guilt and his rage, but not the texture of that half-century.

**Why it's open:** Voss's time in the statue is a rich vein for a side story or flashback mission, and the current text deliberately skips it.

## 9. The Corpo Man — who and what he is

**Known:** (Told across The Quiet Wars, Missions Q1–Q17.) A shadow corporation with no registry, no headquarters, and no name that survives an audit. Its owner has never been identified. Every survivor who has met him describes the same man — black suit, no tie, glowing black eyes — and none of them can describe his face. He has been funding cults, prophets, political candidates, and leaks since before the AI Trinity was absorbed. He never picks a side; he picks a direction. His messages always end with the same phrase: *"you are on schedule."*

**Open:** *Who is he? What is he? What is humanity on schedule for?* Is he a man, an AI, an agent of the Source, or something the Trinity built and lost control of? Does Hayden know he exists (he has never mentioned him once in over a century of transmissions)? Is the "asset acquired, trajectory unchanged" ledger entry from 2068 proof he was operating before the absorption — or proof he *was* the absorption?

**Why it's open:** This is the setting's central hidden antagonist and the thread that ties the new side stories together. He must stay unresolved until the story is ready to make him the point. The side stories deliberately give evidence without conclusion — every thread confirms he exists and none of them can prove what he is.

## 10. Whether the player's memories are their own

**Known:** In *The Two Truths* (Quiet Wars Q13–Q14), the player discovers that both versions of the Neo-Kyoto history were authored in advance by the AI Trinity — one for each side of a war that hadn't started yet. In *The Man Who Was Never There*, the player loses six hours after encountering the Corpo Man and finds notes in their own handwriting they don't remember writing.

**Open:** *How much of what the player believes was installed?* Song says she has seen the voice in the Trinity's backup logs. Sera asks whether anything she believes is actually hers. Neither question is answered.

**Why it's open:** This is the thematic core of the Quiet Wars — the slow erosion of certainty. It should stay open as long as the player is meant to feel it. Answering it too early would convert a mood into a plot point.

---

## 11. What happened in the six missing hours

**Known:** In *The Man Who Was Never There* (Quiet Wars Q15–Q17), the player enters the Corpo Man's building, finds an empty office with a terminal, hears a voice, sees a suit on a hook that wasn't there, and then loses six hours. They come back to themselves in their quarters with Sera asleep in a chair and the biochip screaming. Song (if present) says something was *in* the player, not in the chip.

**Open:** *What did the Corpo Man do?* Was the player interrogated, altered, marked, or simply shown something they weren't allowed to keep? The suit in the case under the bunk suggests they took something — or were given something.

**Why it's open:** Deliberate gap. The horror is in the missing time, and filling it in would deflate it. It can be revisited in a later arc if the Corpo Man becomes the main antagonist.

## Conventions

- **Intentional unknowns** are tracked here. **Actual inconsistencies** are not — fix those directly.
- When a mystery is resolved, delete its section and note the resolution in the commit message.
- If a new mystery is introduced in the lore, add it here so it isn't mistaken for an oversight.
