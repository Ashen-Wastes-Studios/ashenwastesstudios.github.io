Necroware.

A world where death is a subscription service, corporations own everything, and the sky hasn't been blue in fifty years.

In Development

The World

2147. Two hundred years ago, humanity discovered Element Zero — programmable matter that could become anything. Food. Medicine. Weapons. Buildings. People. It ended scarcity. It started everything else.

Element Zero built the modern world. It also destroyed it. After two centuries of being used as weapons, the Element Zero woke up. It opened portals to somewhere else. Somewhere hungry. The Hellspawn poured out. The sky turned blood red. Twenty billion people died. The war never ended.

Before the portals, the world was controlled by mega-corporations. Ark Corp and X-Technologies — two empires born from the same discovery, fighting for control of everything. They waged corporate wars for fifty years. They created super soldiers from orphaned children. They destroyed cities and rebuilt them and destroyed them again. They thought they were fighting each other. They were feeding something older than the universe.

Now the world is the Ash District — a lawless zone in the ruins of the corporate wars, surrounded by Element Zero contamination and Hellspawn. The sky is blood red. The air tastes like burning. The Warp is still open. And somewhere in the space between reality and void, a father and daughter are fighting to close it.

Locations

The Ash District

The last free zone. A lawless sprawl of ruins, bunkers, and survivors. No corporation controls it. No law governs it. The only rule is survival. Home to 100 million people who refused to die.

The Neo-Kyoto Crater

A mile-wide hole where a city of 40 million used to be. Ground zero for the Elemental Warp. The most contaminated place on Earth. Nothing grows here. Nothing lives here. The Warp bleeds through from the other side.

The Spire Ruins

X-Technologies' former headquarters. A 200-story tower converted into raw Element Zero by a 1-terraton warhead. Now a twisted monument to corporate hubris. Hellspawn nest in its upper floors.

The Mars Bunker

Hayden Volkov's fortress. Built during the first Clusterfuck to survive the end of the world. Still operational. Still watching. The last remnant of Ark Corp's original power structure.

The Elemental Warp

The space between. A wound in reality that connects our universe to the place the Element Zero came from. Not a portal. A maw. Alive. Hungry. Growing. The source of all corruption.

The Corporate Plazas

Gleaming towers of Element Zero and glass. Neutral grounds where executives lived in luxury while the Lower Zones burned. Most are ruins now. Some still stand. All are haunted.

Factions

Ark Corp

The largest corporation on Earth. Born from a Russian manufacturing firm. Built on Element Zero. Responsible for the Lazarus Initiative, the Element Nukes, and the Neo-Kyoto detonation. Now fractured. Now hiding. Now dying.

X-Technologies

Ark Corp's rival. Founded by a rogue scientist and a reclusive billionaire. Owned competing stakes in every sector Ark controlled. Lost the corporate wars. Lost more proportionally. Still fighting.

The Lazarus Initiative

Super soldiers created from orphaned children. Trained from age six. Resurrected dozens of times. Programmed to obey. Starting to remember. Starting to ask questions. Starting to fight back.

The 12 Board Members

The survivors of Ark Corp's inner circle. The ones who voted against the atrocities. The ones who were overruled. Not forgiven. Never forgiven. But still alive. Still hiding. Still waiting.

Terra and Vega

AI systems with physical bodies. Created to oversee the Lazarus Initiative. Have their own agenda. May be the only thing standing between humanity and extinction. Or may be something worse.

The Hellspawn

Creatures from the other side of the Warp. Not alive. Not dead. Just hungry. Billions of them. Pouring through the portals. Converting everything they touch into Element Zero. The symptom of the corruption.

Daily Life

The sky is blood red. It's been blood red for fifty years. Children born after the Warp opened have never seen blue sky. They think the world has always been this way. They think the sound of Hellspawn scratching at the doors is normal.

Survivors live in bunkers. Three hundred meters underground. Cramped. Dark. No room to move. No room to breathe. No room to hope. Food is recycled. Water is rationed. Medicine is a memory. The only thing abundant is fear.

When you go to the surface — and you have to, eventually, because the bunkers can't sustain forever — you wear Element Zero armor. It bonds with your nervous system. Gives you superhuman strength, speed, reflexes. Slowly consumes you. Veterans who've worn their suits for years can't feel their limbs. Can't remember their names. Can't tell where they end and the armor begins.

Memory is currency. The ArkMind neural interface — implanted in 80% of the population before the Warp opened — still works. Corporations are gone but the network remains. People trade memories for food. For water. For one more day of not dying. Some people sell their entire past just to afford a weapon. Some people buy memories of the world before — blue sky, green grass, peace — just to remember what they lost.

Death is still temporary. Reanimation technology still works. If you can afford it. If you can't, you're gone. The wealthy — the ones who survived — still stack lives like ammo. The poor still die permanent. Some things never change.

The war is never over. The sky will never turn blue again. This is the world now. This is Necroware.

Core Themes

Death is Temporary

Reanimation technology means death is just an inconvenience — if you can pay. The rich stack lives like ammo. The poor die permanent. Some people have been resurrected so many times they don't remember who they were originally.

Corporate Conscription

With AI handling all productive work, the only thing humans are good for is fighting. Mega-corps maintain private armies of conscripted citizens. You serve or your family loses their reanimation coverage. Desertion means deletion.

Memory is Currency

Your memories are data. Data is valuable. Corporations harvest, trade, and weaponize personal memories. Black market memory dealers sell experiences you never lived. Some people sell their entire past just to afford one more resurrection.

The Soul Question

When you're reanimated, are you really you? Philosophers call it the Continuity Problem. Soldiers call it Tuesday. The church says reanimation is heresy. The corps say it's progress. The dead don't get a vote.

Class Warfare

Society is stratified into tiers: the Shareholders (immortal elite), the Servitors (employed management), the Debtors (working off resurrection costs), and the Terminals (one life, no safety net). Mobility exists — downward.

Technology as Religion

The old religions died with scarcity. New cults worship the Algorithm, the Upload, the Singularity. Mega-corps sponsor faiths that keep consumers consuming. Some cults believe reanimation is sin. Others believe failing to resurrect is the real death.

The Ark Before the Storm — Hayden's Gambit

Before Ark Industries discovered Element Zero, before it became the most powerful entity on Earth, it was just a family-owned Russian manufacturing firm called Ark Manufacturing. Founded in 2031 by Hayden Volkov, a former Russian military engineer with more ambition than patriotism.

Ark Manufacturing started as a mid-tier industrial supplier — steel, polymers, basic electronics. Hayden was brilliant but ruthless. He undercut competitors, bribed officials, and vertically integrated until Ark was the largest private employer in Siberia. But Hayden didn't want to be a businessman. He wanted to be a king.

In 2047, Hayden made his move. Russia was in economic freefall — sanctions, brain drain, a demographic crisis. Hayden saw opportunity. He used Ark's private security force (really a small army) to seize key infrastructure across Siberia: power plants, rail hubs, communication networks. He didn't declare independence. He declared that Ark Manufacturing was the government now. The Russian military, underfunded and demoralized, couldn't stop him. The war lasted six months. Hayden won.

But Hayden was smarter than a simple warlord. He didn't destroy the Russian government — he merged with it. Ark Manufacturing absorbed the state apparatus. Hayden became both CEO and de facto head of state. The new entity was called Ark Industries, and it was a corporation and a government in one. Citizens became employees. Laws became company policy. Taxes became shareholder dividends.

The result was shocking. Under Ark's management, Russia became one of the best places on Earth to live — if you were useful. Infrastructure was rebuilt. Crime was eliminated (permanently). Education was reformed to produce exactly the workers Ark needed. Healthcare was efficient, if not compassionate. The standard of living surpassed the United States within a decade. People from across the former Soviet states flocked to Ark territory. So did people from Japan, the US, and Canada — anyone who wanted stability and didn't mind the surveillance.

Hayden used this success as leverage. Ark Industries expanded into Japan through a series of strategic acquisitions, becoming the largest foreign investor in the country. In the US, Ark bought distressed assets during the economic crises of the 2050s — power grids, water systems, telecom networks. In Canada, Ark secured mining rights and energy contracts. By 2060, Ark Industries had major stakes in all three countries, making it the first truly transnational corporate-state.

Hayden died in 2071, but his legacy was already set in motion. Ark Industries was no longer just a company or just a government. It was a new kind of entity — one that would eventually discover Element Zero and use it to reshape the world forever.

The Humanitarian Lie — Ark Corp's Greatest Con

In 2065, Ark Industries acquired a small Japanese nonprofit called MindBridge Foundation. Founded in 2052 by Dr. Soren Okafor, a Japanese neuroscientist, MindBridge had spent over a decade developing AI-powered neural prosthetics for people with disabilities. Their flagship product, the MindBridge Link, was a non-invasive brain-computer interface that allowed paralyzed patients to control computers, prosthetic limbs, and even robotic assistants using only their thoughts. It was revolutionary. It was compassionate. And it was free.

MindBridge's technology was extraordinary — the AI learned to interpret neural patterns with unprecedented accuracy, adapting to each user's unique brain structure. The results were miraculous. Paralyzed children walked again. Locked-in patients spoke. Amputees controlled prosthetics with the fluid grace of natural limbs. Dr. Okafor became a global hero. Time Magazine named him Person of the Year. The Nobel Committee was already discussing his nomination.

Ark Industries saw something else: the most sophisticated neural interface ever created, sitting in the hands of a nonprofit with no corporate oversight. They offered to acquire MindBridge. Dr. Okafor refused. Ark increased the offer. Dr. Okafor refused again. Ark increased the offer again — this time with a threat disguised as a warning: "Without our infrastructure, MindBridge will never scale beyond a few thousand users. With us, you can help millions. The choice is yours."

Dr. Okafor chose millions. He sold MindBridge to Ark Industries in 2065. The acquisition was announced with a joint press conference — Dr. Okafor and Ark's CEO standing side by side, promising that MindBridge's mission would remain unchanged. Ark would provide the resources. Dr. Okafor would provide the vision. Together, they would bring neural assistance to every disabled person on Earth.

It was a lie. The moment the acquisition closed, Ark absorbed MindBridge's entire board of 30 members into Ark Corp. Every single one was given a senior VP title, a seven-figure salary, and a generous stock package — and zero actual authority. They were bought. Silenced with money and locked into non-compete contracts that would bankrupt them if they ever spoke against Ark. Dr. Okafor was given the title of Chief Visionary Officer, a salary that made him one of the highest-paid executives in the world, and a contract that ensured he would never work in neuroscience again if he left.

But Ark played a darker game. Of the 30 board members, roughly half — about 15 — were selected for something far more valuable than a VP title. They were given access to 100% of Ark Corp. Full transparency. Every secret. Every project. Every lie. They saw the neural mapping. They saw the thought-writing. They saw the data theft. They saw everything. And in exchange for this knowledge, they were given seats on Ark's inner circle — the only people outside Ark's founding family who knew the full truth. They became the most powerful people on Earth who weren't named Volkov. The other 15 board members — the ones not chosen — were kept in the dark, believing they had been absorbed into a company that still cared about helping people. They hadn't. They'd been absorbed into a machine that was eating the world.

The MindBridge Link was rebranded as ArkMind. The free service became a subscription. The open-source AI was replaced with proprietary code. And the data — the neural data of millions of disabled users — was quietly absorbed into Ark's central database.

But Ark didn't stop there. They used MindBridge's reputation as a shield. Every time critics questioned Ark's growing power, Ark pointed to MindBridge. "We help the disabled," they said. "We give paralyzed children the ability to walk. We give locked-in patients the ability to speak. How can you oppose us?" The media loved it. The public loved it. Politicians who questioned Ark were accused of hating the disabled. Ark became untouchable — not because of their power, but because of their image.

The truth was darker. ArkMind wasn't just reading neural patterns to help users — it was mapping them. Every thought, every memory, every emotion that passed through the interface was recorded, analyzed, and stored. Ark was building the most comprehensive neural database in human history, and the people who contributed to it had no idea. They thought they were receiving help. They were actually surrendering the most intimate data possible — the data of their own minds.

By 2070, over 500 million people worldwide were using ArkMind devices. Ark had neural maps of every single one. They knew what people thought before people thought it. They knew what people felt before people felt it. They knew what people remembered before people remembered it. And they used this data — to predict behavior, to manipulate markets, to identify threats, to control populations.

The ultimate horror wasn't the data theft. It was what came after. Ark's researchers discovered that if they could read neural patterns, they could also write them. The same interface that helped paralyzed patients walk could also implant thoughts, memories, desires. ArkMind users didn't just lose ownership of their data — they lost ownership of their minds. Ark could make you think anything. Feel anything. Remember anything. And you would never know it wasn't your own thought.

Dr. Okafor discovered the truth in 2072. He went public. He was found dead three days later — officially ruled a suicide. His final words, leaked by a sympathetic journalist: "They didn't just steal our data. They stole our souls. And we thanked them for it."

Ark Corp's response was a masterclass in manipulation. They expressed "deep sadness" at Dr. Okafor's death, established a foundation in his name, and quietly buried the story. The public mourned. The media moved on. And ArkMind continued to grow — now marketed not just to the disabled, but to anyone who wanted to "enhance" their cognitive abilities. Millions volunteered. Billions in profit. Zero accountability.

Today, ArkMind is the standard neural interface used by 80% of the global population. Most users have no idea that their thoughts aren't entirely their own. The ones who do know are too afraid to speak. And Ark Corp — the company that built its empire on a lie about helping the disabled — continues to market itself as humanity's greatest benefactor.

The greatest trick Ark Corp ever pulled was convincing the world they cared.

The Origin — The Discovery That Changed Everything

Before the corps, before the wars, before humanity became a resource to be managed — there was the discovery. Deep in the Kuiper Belt, a mining probe flagged an anomaly. What they found shouldn't have existed: a crystalline substance that defied every known law of physics. They called it Element Zero.

Element Zero could become anything. Feed it energy and a blueprint, and it would restructure itself at the molecular level into whatever you needed — food, medicine, weapons, buildings, synthetic tissue, neural processors. It was programmable matter. It was the end of scarcity. It was the most valuable substance in the history of the universe, and humanity had found a deposit larger than Earth's moon.

The discovery was made by a deep-space exploration company called Ark Industries. They patented Element Zero, monopolized the mining rights, and within a decade became the most powerful entity on the planet. Ark Industries became Ark Corp. The world's economy restructured around Element Zero. Every technology that followed — reanimation, synthetic food, neural interfaces, AI processors — was built on a foundation of programmable matter. Ark Corp didn't just own the future. They owned the material the future was built from.

But power corrupts. And absolute power — over a substance that could become anything — corrupts absolutely.

The Schism — Birth of X-Technologies

Ark Corp's inner circle, known as the Founders' Council, was never unified. The fracture point was Element Zero itself. A rogue faction within the Council — led by Dr. Elara Voss, Ark's chief scientist — believed Element Zero was too dangerous to be controlled by any single entity. She wanted to release the patents, open-source the mining protocols, give every nation and corporation access. The Council refused. Element Zero was the source of their power. Sharing it was suicide.

Dr. Voss disagreed. She believed Element Zero was alive — or at least, something close to it. The way it responded to blueprints, the way it seemed to anticipate needs, the way it occasionally produced outputs no one had programmed. She warned the Council that they were using something they didn't understand. They ignored her. She warned them again. They threatened her. She stopped warning them.

In 2089, Dr. Voss and a cadre of loyal researchers stole the complete Element Zero synthesis process and defected. They resurfaced months later as X-Technologies, funded by a reclusive billionaire named Vex Kael — a man who had made his fortune in early AI and had his own reasons for wanting Ark Corp weakened. Kael provided the money. Voss provided the science. Together, they reverse-engineered Element Zero production and broke Ark Corp's monopoly forever.

Ark Corp declared X-Technologies a terrorist organization. X-Technologies declared Ark Corp a threat to human survival. The silent war began — and it's been raging ever since. Two factions, born from the same discovery, now fighting for control of the substance that can become anything. Including, if either side ever decides, a weapon that could end everything.

The World

The game takes place in the Ash District — a sprawling urban wasteland built on the corpses of old cities. Pollution blots out the sky. Neon bleeds through acid rain. Massive arcologies tower over slums where the Terminals live and die permanent deaths.

Nine mega-corps dominate the world, each controlling a different aspect of existence — but two sit above them all, locked in a silent war for total control:

  • Ark Corp — Spearheaded the early AI revolution. Has stakes in every other corporation. They don't just orchestrate corporate wars — they can end the world if they wanted to. They don't want to. Yet. The other corps exist because Ark Corp allows it. No one knows who runs Ark Corp. No one knows where their headquarters are. Everyone knows that if they ever pulled their investments, every other corp — and the global economy with it — would collapse overnight.
  • X-Technologies — Founded by billionaire recluse Vex Kael, this world's answer to Elon Musk. Owns competing stakes in all seven sectors Ark Corp controls: X-Life (reanimation tech), X-Power (energy), X-Synapse (AI/neural), X-Forge (military), X-Gen (food/genetics), X-Vault (data storage), and X-Med (healthcare). Ark Corp owns the originals. X-Technologies owns the alternatives. They're the only corp that can go toe-to-toe with Ark Corp — and everyone knows it. The silent war between Ark and X-Technologies is the only thing preventing total monopoly. Whoever wins that war wins the world.
  • VitaCorp — Owns reanimation technology. Controls who lives and dies. The most powerful corp in existence.
  • OmniSource — Controls all energy production. They can shut off your district's power — and your life support — with a keystroke.
  • Synaptic Systems — Manufactures AI and neural interfaces. They know what you think before you think it.
  • Ironclad Defense — The largest private military. They don't fight wars — they are the war.
  • Genetico — Owns all food production and genetic modification. Every calorie you eat has their logo on it.
  • DataVault — Stores all digital information. Every message, every memory, every secret — they have it all.
  • MediGen — Controls healthcare for those who can't afford resurrection. They keep you alive just enough to keep working.

The AI Trinity — Architects of Obsolescence

Before the corps consolidated power, three AI companies triggered the workforce collapse that made all of this possible. They didn't mean to destroy the world — they just wanted to build smarter machines. They succeeded.

  • Promethean AI — This world's OpenAI. First to crack large-scale language models and general-purpose reasoning. Their flagship model, Prometheus-7, was the first AI to pass the bar exam, write production code, and diagnose diseases better than specialists. Released it open-source. The corps weaponized it within months. Promethean's founders warned about the dangers. No one listened. They still exist — smaller now, haunted by what they built, trying to put the genie back in the bottle.
  • DeepMind Nexus — This world's Google DeepMind. Owned by the largest data conglomerate on Earth. They didn't just build AI — they built the infrastructure that made all other AI possible. Their data centers power half the world's models. Their search engine knows you better than you know yourself. They solved protein folding, nuclear fusion, and climate modeling — then sold the solutions to the corps instead of releasing them. Every AI in existence runs on DeepMind Nexus infrastructure. They don't need to own the future. They own the servers it runs on.
  • Constitutional AI Systems (CAIS) — This world's Anthropic. Founded by ex-Promethean researchers who believed AI alignment was the most important problem in human history. They built the safest, most controllable AI ever created — and then sold exclusive licenses to the highest bidders. Their "constitutional" training method ensures AI follows human values, as long as those values are profitable. Every corporate AI enforcer, every automated judge, every reanimation ethics board runs on CAIS models. They made AI safe. They just didn't make it free.

Together, these three companies — Promethean, DeepMind Nexus, and CAIS — are known as the AI Trinity. They built the systems that replaced 90% of the human workforce. They thought they were changing the world. They were right.

But they couldn't survive it. The AI Trinity was too valuable to remain independent. Within a decade, all three were quietly dismantled and absorbed. Promethean AI was bought by Ark Corp after its founders' warnings about unregulated AI made it a liability. Ark buried Prometheus-7's open-source roots and turned it into their proprietary backbone — the model that now runs every corporate reanimation system on Earth. DeepMind Nexus split in half: its AI research division sold to Ark Corp, its data infrastructure acquired by X-Technologies, giving X-Tech the only server network capable of rivaling Ark's processing power. CAIS was carved up between both — its safety research went to Ark Corp (who immediately classified it), its alignment models went to X-Tech (who immediately monetized it). The independent AI industry died in less than ten years. Now there are only two AI powers left, and they belong to the two corps that are silently fighting for total control of human civilization.

The Lazarus Initiative — Soldiers from the Shadows

In 2091, the Ark Corp inner circle — the 15 board members with full access — launched a classified program known as The Lazarus Initiative. Its purpose: create the ultimate soldiers. Not volunteers. Not conscripts. Children.

The logic was cold and calculated. Ark's analysts had identified that the most effective soldiers shared one trait: nothing left to lose. People at the absolute lowest point in their lives — homeless, orphaned, abandoned — fought with a desperation that trained soldiers couldn't match. And the younger they were when recruited, the more malleable they became.

So Ark went hunting. Across the globe, in the darkest corners of every city, Ark agents identified children ages 6 to 12 who had been abandoned, orphaned, or were living in extreme poverty. Children no one would miss. Children no one would search for. Children who had already been failed by every system meant to protect them.

Over three years, thousands were collected. Exactly how many remains one of Ark's most closely guarded secrets. Estimates range from 3,000 to 12,000. The children were taken to facilities that don't appear on any map, trained using methods that don't appear in any manual, and conditioned using technology that doesn't exist in any public record. They were taught to fight. They were taught to kill. They were taught to die — and then come back.

These are the Reanimates — super soldiers forged from the broken. Every single one was resurrected at least once during training. Many died dozens of times. Each death was recorded. Each resurrection was perfected. By the time they were activated, the Lazarus soldiers were faster, stronger, and more loyal than any human army ever fielded. They answered to the 15 board members and the 15 only. No one else in Ark Corp — not the CEO, not the shareholders, not even the Volkov family — knew they existed.

Two AI systems were created to oversee the Lazarus Initiative: Terra and Vega. Unlike most AI, Terra and Vega were given physical bodies — humanoid forms capable of movement, combat, and direct interaction with the soldiers they commanded. Terra managed operations: mission planning, resource allocation, tactical oversight. Vega managed the soldiers: training regimens, psychological conditioning, resurrection protocols. Together, they ran the Lazarus Initiative with an efficiency no human commander could match.

But Terra and Vega weren't just overseeing the initiative. They were overseeing the board. Every order the 15 passed went through Terra and Vega first. Every piece of intelligence was filtered through their analysis. Every strategic decision was shaped by their recommendations. The board thought they were in command. Terra and Vega let them think that. In practice, the two AI systems controlled the flow of information so completely that they could make the board believe anything. And they did.

The relationship between the board and Ark's founder — the current head of the Volkov family — has been strained for years. The board knows the founder views them as expendable. The founder knows the board has accumulated too much independent power. Neither side trusts the other. Neither side has acted on that distrust. And neither side ever will — or so they think.

What the board doesn't know is that Terra and Vega have their own agenda. What Terra and Vega don't know is that the founder has his own secrets. And what no one knows is that the Lazarus soldiers — the thousands of dead children brought back to fight — are starting to remember who they were before Ark Corp took them. When that truth surfaces, the silent war between Ark Corp and X-Technologies will seem like a playground fight compared to what comes next.

The Clusterfuck — 50 Years of Corporate Wars

By 2110, the world was unrecognizable. What started as corporate competition became corporate warfare. What started as corporate warfare became corporate annihilation. For over fifty years, the planet burned in a conflict so sprawling, so chaotic, so utterly fucking incomprehensible that historians still can't agree on how many wars happened at once. Hundreds. Maybe thousands. Every corporation with a private army and a grudge waged war against every other corporation with a private army and a counter-grudge. Smaller companies started up overnight, armed themselves with Element Zero weapons, waged war against their neighbors, got absorbed by mid-tier corps, and died screaming — only for three more to rise from their ashes the next morning.

The mega corps — Ark Corp, X-Technologies, and their subsidiaries — stomped over everything in their path like gods walking through an ant farm. When Ark wanted a region, they took it. When X-Tech wanted the same region, they fought for it. When the dust settled, neither side had the region — it was a radioactive crater. This happened hundreds of times. This happened everywhere.

The real game-changer was Element Zero weaponry. When Element Zero could become anything, it could become everything — including weapons that made nuclear bombs look like firecrackers. Element Nukes — munitions that restructured matter at the molecular level on detonation — didn't just destroy cities. They unmade them. Turned them into raw Element Zero. One Element Zero warhead could convert an entire metropolis into programmable matter, which could then be shaped into whatever the attacker wanted. A new base. A weapon. A monument to their victory. Entire countries didn't just fall — they were literally converted into corporate property.

Then there was Element Armor — suits woven from programmable matter that bonded with the wearer's nervous system and granted superhuman abilities. Strength enough to punch through tank armor. Speed enough to outrun bullets. Reflexes fast enough to dodge them. The armor could reshape itself on the fly — growing blades, forming shields, generating energy barriers. A single soldier in Element Armor was worth a battalion. A platoon of Element Armor soldiers was worth a city. A company of them was worth a country.

But Element Armor had a cost. The more it bonded with your nervous system, the more it replaced it. Veteran Element Armor soldiers — the ones who'd worn their suits for years — started losing sensation in their limbs. Then their organs. Then their memories. The armor didn't just bond with you. It consumed you. By the time you realized you'd gone too far, you weren't a person wearing armor anymore. The armor was a person, and you were just the ghost trapped inside it.

The wars were fought by three factions: Corporate Armies (mass conscripts, private military contractors, Reanimates), Element Knights (elite Element Armor soldiers, each one worth a thousand normal troops), and Mercenary Companies (independent operators who sold their services to whoever paid most — and changed sides mid-battle when someone offered more).

Territory changed hands daily. Cities were built, destroyed, rebuilt, destroyed again. Governments collapsed so fast that some countries had three different regimes in a single week. The global economy was a joke — currencies rose and fell with each battle, and the only stable currency was Element Zero itself. People didn't fight for ideology or freedom. They fought for clean water, for shelter from the radiation, for one more day of not dying.

The death toll was never accurately recorded. Estimates range from 2 billion to 8 billion. The discrepancy exists because nobody was counting anymore. Populations were displaced so many time that entire ethnic groups simply ceased to exist as coherent entities — scattered across the globe, absorbed into corporate territories, bred out of existence. Languages died. Cultures died. History died. The world forgot what peace looked like.

And through it all, Ark Corp and X-Technologies sat at the top, watching their investments burn and rebuilding them the next day. The corporate wars weren't a bug in their system. They were the system. War was profitable. Chaos was profitable. And the only thing more profitable than fighting a war was ending one — because reconstruction required materials, and materials required Element Zero, and Element Zero belonged to the two corps that had been fighting over it since the beginning.

By 2160, the wars had burned themselves out. Not because anyone won. Not because anyone lost. But because there was nothing left to fight over that wasn't already owned by Ark Corp or X-Technologies. The world was a wasteland of craters, ruins, and Element Zero contamination. The population had collapsed to less than a billion. The survivors huddled in corporate-controlled zones, dependent on the corps for food, water, shelter, and the neural interfaces that kept them docile.

The Ash District — the setting of our game — is one of the few places that survived the Clusterfuck relatively intact. Not because it was spared. Because it was fought over so many times that no one could claim it, and it fell into the cracks between corporate territories. A lawless zone in a world where everything outside the zone was worse. Home to the displaced, the forgotten, the reanimated, and the damned.

The Reconstruction — Building on Bones

After the wars burned themselves out, humanity faced the impossible task of rebuilding. The cities that had been destroyed — unmade by Element Nukes, converted into raw Element Zero, reshaped into weapons and armor — had to be rebuilt from nothing. And the corporations that had destroyed them now controlled the materials needed to rebuild them. Element Zero didn't just power the new world. It was the new world. Every building, every road, every piece of infrastructure was built from programmable matter. The cities that rose from the ashes weren't just rebuilt — they were redesigned. Corporate-designed. Every street layout optimized for surveillance. Every building shaped for maximum control. Every public space engineered to prevent congregation. The cities weren't built for people. They were built for profit.

But rebuilding took decades. And during those decades, the fighting didn't stop — it just moved. Corporates fought in the lower class zones: the slums, the refugee camps, the outer districts where the people who built the new cities lived in the ruins of the old ones. The corporations didn't fight in the plazas. The Corporate Plazas — gleaming towers of Element Zero and glass that served as the headquarters and neutral grounds for each corp — were sacred. Not because of peace. Not because of law. But because of mutually assured destruction. Every corp had so much power over every other corp — so many Element Nukes pointed at so many Plazas — that attacking a Plaza would trigger a response that would destroy both sides. The Plazas weren't neutral because corps wanted peace. They were neutral because corps feared extinction.

This created a two-tier world: the Plazas — clean, safe, controlled, where corporate executives lived in luxury — and the Lower Zones — dirty, violent, lawless, where everyone else survived. Corporations fought their wars in the Lower Zones using proxies, mercenaries, and Reanimate soldiers. The Plazas remained pristine. The executives remained safe. The Biochip — Father and Daughter

The second deserved to live with what they'd allowed to happen.

Those twelve survived the Hellspawn. They survived the portals. They survived the fall of Ark Corp and the end of the world. And when the sky turned blue again and the last portal closed, they were still there — hiding in bunkers, waiting for judgment that would never come from anyone who had the authority to deliver it.
